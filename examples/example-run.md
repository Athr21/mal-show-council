# Example run: raise now, or run on profits for 12 more months?

**This is an illustrative run.** The company, the numbers and the founder are invented to show the council's shape. No real company is described. Guests' own public companies are named only where a seat cites its source episode.

## The question as the founder asked it

"We are a 60 person software and services company in Cairo, profitable but thin. Two regional funds have offered a Series A. Growth is slowing. Do we take the money now or push on profits for another year and raise from strength?"

## Decision

By end of this quarter: (a) accept a Series A now, or (b) decline, run on profits for 12 months and revisit, or (c) take a smaller bridge from existing angels and family and revisit in six months.

Seats that matter most here: Treasurer, Capital Sceptic, Safety Net. The Ambassador has a view on the Riyadh part. The Conscience will likely pass on the money question and speak only to hiring.

## Goal and numbers

On the founder's figures (below): option (a) buys roughly 18 months of the current burn plus three new bets; option (b) at 15 percent growth keeps the company profitable but leaves 3.5 months of payroll exposed to any currency move, since 70 percent of revenue is in pounds and 40 percent of costs in dollars; option (c) covers the exposure but funds none of the growth. The stated goal is "raise from strength". The real goal, on the founder's own words, is to be in Riyadh before a competitor. That is a market decision wearing a funding decision's clothes.

## Missing facts

1. How many months of payroll are in the bank today, counting only cash that has arrived, not invoices?
2. What share of revenue is in Egyptian pounds versus dollars or dirhams, and what share of costs?
3. What exactly would the Series A buy, line by line, and what does the growth plan look like with zero new money?

Founder's answers (invented for this run): 3.5 months of payroll in cash; 70 percent of revenue in pounds, 40 percent of costs in dollars; the round would fund a Riyadh office, 15 hires and a product rebuild; without it, growth stays at roughly 15 percent a year.

## Treasurer

Position: take money, but not necessarily this money. 3.5 months of payroll with a 70 percent pound revenue base and 40 percent dollar costs is one currency move away from a crisis. You are not raising from strength; you are raising from exposure.
Risk: a devaluation or a single large customer paying 90 days late pushes you below two months of payroll while you are mid negotiation, and the terms get worse the day the funds notice. (Episode 8, 6)
Question back: who are your three biggest receivables and how late are they today?
What would change my mind: six months of payroll in the bank and receivables under 45 days. Then option (b) is real.

## Capital Sceptic

Position: do not sign until you can say what each dollar buys and what each fund needs to call this a success. A Riyadh office, 15 hires and a rebuild is three bets, not one. A venture fund needs you to become an outlier; a 15 percent growth services business is not that, and both sides will feel the mismatch in year two. (Episode 7, 5)
Risk: you take venture money for a business that should be taking strategic or credit money, and the pressure to grow into the valuation breaks what works.
Question back: have either of these funds operated a company in Saudi, and will they open doors there, or only wire money?
What would change my mind: a fund with real Gulf operating experience and a round sized to one bet, the Riyadh office, with the rebuild funded from profits.

## Safety Net

Position: option (c) or a small (a). The founder has 3.5 months of runway for 60 families and, from the follow up, most of their personal savings in the company. That is one bet with no backup. Any raise should first restore a buffer that is not deployed. (Episode 6, 10)
Risk: convertible notes and liquidation preferences signed in a hurry. The upside of a good outcome gets cut later by terms nobody read now. Get a lawyer before the term sheet, not after. (Episode 6)
Question back: if this company closed in nine months, what would you and your co-founder live on?
What would change my mind: a personal safety net separate from the company, and a term sheet reviewed by counsel.

## Conscience

Passes on the money question: either path is fine on principle. One risk only: hiring 15 people fast lowers the bar and changes the culture that produced the profit. What would change my mind: a growth plan that names the customer problem first and the fundraising second. (Episode 4, 3)

## Long View

Position: the regional pattern says profitable companies with options outlive funded companies without them, and the funding headline is not the pipeline. But the pattern is not a law. Riyadh is a real opportunity with a window. (Episode 8, 7, 10)
Risk: waiting 12 months to "raise from strength" while a competitor raises now and takes Riyadh. Momentum is hard to get back. Also, launching Riyadh with a rebuild at the same time doubles the learning load, which is exactly the mistake a scaler on this show admits to. (Episode 10)
Question back: what did the last two Egyptian companies that opened in Riyadh learn about licensing, labour and local partners, and have you spoken to them?
What would change my mind: evidence that the Riyadh window closes within a year. Then the raise is urgent.

## Ambassador

Position: Riyadh is won on the ground, not from Cairo. Before any office, the founder needs two or three Saudi buyers who will take a call and say who else to talk to. If those names do not exist yet, the office is a cost with no story. (Episode 10, 5)
Risk: a playbook copied from Cairo. Positioning and relationships differ city by city, and the one company on this show that expanded abroad learned the labour rules while already trading. (Episode 10)
Question back: who in Riyadh would vouch for you today, by name, if a buyer called them?
What would change my mind: one signed Saudi client, however small, before the office opens. Then the Riyadh bet is a real bet and the raise can be sized to it.

## Where they disagree

- Treasurer and Long View both want Riyadh, but the Treasurer wants the buffer first and the Long View fears the window. They cannot both be fully satisfied; one of them is accepting a real risk.
- Capital Sceptic and Safety Net agree the money should be smaller and cleaner, but disagree on the source: the Sceptic wants strategic or credit, the Safety Net is comfortable with family and angels if the terms are simple.
- Conscience is alone in treating the hiring plan as the biggest risk; the others treat it as a line item.
- Ambassador and Long View both doubt the Riyadh office as planned, but for opposite reasons: the Ambassador says the relationships come first, the Long View says the window may not wait for them.

## Chair's call

Take a smaller round, from one fund with Gulf operating experience, sized to the Riyadh office plus six months of untouched payroll buffer. Fund the product rebuild from profits over 18 months, sequenced after Riyadh is live. Decline the full round.

Confidence: medium. The facts support the call, but one event could reverse it within the month: if the receivables below are badly aged, the Treasurer's position becomes the call.

Check this week: pull the receivables ledger and age it. If more than 30 percent of receivables are over 60 days, the Treasurer's position becomes the call and the raise gets bigger and faster.
