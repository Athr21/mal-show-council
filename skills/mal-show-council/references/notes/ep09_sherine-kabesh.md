# Ep 9 · Sherine Kabesh

Sherine Kabesh studied finance, spent her career in marketing, and founded Flash, a fintech in Egypt focused on payments and financial wellness. The conversation covers being a woman in male-dominated rooms, how she de-risked her own leap into a startup, practical budgeting, what financial freedom means to her, ethical investing, and trust in data, credit and QR payments.

## Key takeaways

- [00:02:55] Her views have shifted over time. The job itself is not the hard part for women; the social side is, such as being the only woman on a set or explaining to a partner a meeting with 20 men. Banking and fintech have long had few women in middle and senior roles.
- [00:04:27] She rejects the idea that women are more emotional or more emotionally intelligent at work; the practical barrier is the life outside work.
- [00:05:25] Her finance classes were balanced, and the top of the class was often two women competing, yet she cannot find many of them now; they went into corporates and she doubts many start companies.
- [00:06:43] Finance is horizontal, not a separate department. Even choosing a celebrity for an advert is a budget and P&L decision, and you do not need a finance degree to have financial awareness.
- [00:07:54] Financial wellness starts with knowing what is in your bank and pocket, so there are fewer surprises, then a short to medium plan for what to save, invest and spend.
- [00:08:55] She is not against buy now, pay later, but asks why you need it now. Give impulse buying its own fixed budget rather than letting it run loose.
- [00:10:17] There is no fixed salary split; it depends on lifestyle and goals. Early in a career, living with parents is a cultural advantage that lets you sort necessities from nice-to-haves and save for things like a car.
- [00:11:49] Before leaving a good Dubai salary for her startup, she spent a year planning while still employed and bought real estate as a safety net, because startups fail often even when you believe in yours.
- [00:12:50] Founding meant dropping a lifestyle of travel and buying; she now tells friends she cannot spend on a restaurant and joins them anyway.
- [00:14:22] Ignore social pressure in every part of life. Save because you have a goal bigger than impressing a friend; others do not see that an early-stage founder makes no money, and style does not need designer labels.
- [00:16:54] Her definition of financial freedom is earning without working, through a spread of assets such as gold, crypto, real estate, equity in a trusted founder's startup, fractional ownership of a truck or agriculture.
- [00:18:00] Freedom still means an active brain: if needed she could return to high-value work, and she would keep watching assets and moving into new industries. Diversify across sectors and across countries to cut risk.
- [00:19:36] Put money only where you personally and religiously believe the source is good, not just where it earns. Define what ethical means to you, or endless digging will show the whole world as bad.
- [00:22:03] On an S&P 500 fund: personally acceptable as a global norm, but through a religious lens some underlying activities are not. Not everything is black and white, so research and ask people of knowledge.
- [00:23:22] The same question applies to ordinary bank certificates versus an Islamic bank; some choices are personal and some are strictly religious.
- [00:24:23] Flash's wellness product compares your spending with people of a similar lifestyle, then nudges you, for example to eat at home and move the burger money into fractional gold, or to look into a high electricity bill.
- [00:25:40] For merchants, digital payments create a financial record that lets a lender extend credit to expand the business.
- [00:26:46] Financial data is tightly regulated: a licence means a long process with the central bank. Flash says it uses data to help customers save rather than push consumption, keeps it encrypted and works mostly with generic behaviour data, not named profiles.
- [00:29:23] Credit carries risk like everything else. Borrowing needs a purpose, ideally growth, and both sides carry risk: the merchant can fall into debt and the lender may not be repaid, so lenders must ask why and how much.
- [00:31:13] Trust in QR payments is a global issue that takes time. It grows when national rails like InstaPay and wallets use QR too, and when payer and merchant get instant notifications and a receipt; Uber went through the same journey.
- [00:33:14] Flash is launching a soundbox, a QR-optimised point-of-sale device that also takes cards, to make QR acceptance feel safe and official.
- [00:34:29] She sees digital payments like electricity or water rather than a risky dependency. Cash also fails when the till or power is down, and digital systems keep the record when they come back.
- [00:35:59] On marketing Uber rides, she argues tracked trips are safer than taxis, where people rarely note the plate, so she is proud of that responsibility while regretting the incidents that happen.
- [00:37:36] First step to financial wellness: list every expense in a simple spreadsheet or on paper, budget the necessities, save even a small amount, and when you overspend in a month, recalculate and carry on.

## Where it fits the council

- Treasurer: write down every expense, budget necessities first, give impulse spending a fixed cap, and borrow only for growth.
- Capital Sceptic: credit needs a purpose; ask why you need the money and how much, because risk sits with both borrower and lender.
- Safety Net: plan a startup for a year while still employed and build a solid asset first; diversify across sectors and countries.
- Conscience: invest only where you personally and religiously accept the source, and use customer data for wellness, not for pushing consumption.
- Long View: financial freedom as assets that earn while you keep your skills sharp as a fallback.
- Ambassador: trust in new payment methods grows through receipts, instant notifications, national rails and time, as it did with Uber.
