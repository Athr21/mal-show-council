# Ep 14 · Dr. Vishnu Katara

Vishnu Katara is a finance leader who helped take Flipkart, Ola's fleet business and Noon through hypergrowth, and now runs the SME business at MAL, an AI-native, Sharia-compliant fintech that was still pre-launch at recording. He explains why banks underserve small businesses, how data-led underwriting could change that, and how MAL frames Sharia compliance and ethics. He also covers AI-assisted hiring, angel investing, when to hire a controller or a CFO, and why a startup job has no job description.

## Key takeaways

- [00:03:30] His corporate roles felt like startups because they were greenfield: he was the first hire on a refinery project that took about three years to launch, then on a telecom towers company assembled from three shareholders' assets.
- [00:05:42] To be entrepreneurial inside a large company, ask for project work rather than steady-state work; you get less autonomy than in a startup but far more learning than in run-the-business roles.
- [00:07:25] Flipkart, Ola and Noon each fought a Western giant and won through local understanding, hustle and caring about the people they served.
- [00:10:22] A company spread across some 200 countries cannot understand a local problem as well as one built from inside a single large market, which can then take the solution global.
- [00:12:48] He came to the UAE in 2019 for "a conversation" and stayed; near-total internet penetration and high capital per head meant the gap was technology intent and founders, and the region has become a startup hub within five to seven years.
- [00:14:18] He credits the UAE's rise mainly to committed leadership that delivers: free zones and hubs, supportive policy and regulation, followed by global firms moving their headquarters in.
- [00:17:12] He moved from CFO to running a business line because a CFO improves one company, while serving SMEs lets him apply financial know-how across thousands of businesses.
- [00:18:28] The paradox he is working on: SMEs make up most of the economy and most new jobs but get only a small slice of funding, and most of their credit applications are refused outright.
- [00:19:38] Banks drifted from serving business needs to managing risk with rules decades old; if they do not understand a business they will not fund it, and the customer must fit the bank's product.
- [00:23:08] Cash-cycle lending: if a lender sees the flows, it can predict when a business needs money (for example ahead of an e-commerce firm's big fourth quarter) and offer a limit upfront, provided it is transparent and ethical about terms.
- [00:25:32] The vision is a set of agents handling collections, supplier payments, payroll and credit gaps; he is candid that this is a phased build and much will not exist at launch.
- [00:31:15] Banks take weeks to months to approve credit because people do the underwriting, and that cannot scale to hundreds of thousands of SMEs; automated underwriting cuts both cost and time. Digitising old workflows is not the same as intelligent banking.
- [00:33:58] MAL's main target is businesses of up to about 100 staff: first a business account, then credit, which he calls the fuel for growth.
- [00:35:32] He wants the lender to fit the borrower, not the reverse, and to treat credit as fungible across early discounting, overdraft, bullet loans and card spend.
- [00:36:58] The red flag for any SME seeking finance is not being straight about its numbers; honesty about figures and intent is what lets a data-driven system back you.
- [00:39:22] He argues Sharia-compliant fees differ from interest because the full cost is stated upfront with no compounding penalties layered on top; Sharia is the floor and ethics the ceiling.
- [00:41:00] Without penalties, the lever for repayment is information: tell the borrower that one missed payment damages their record and can turn a one-minute credit decision into weeks of manual review.
- [00:44:42] MAL publishes roles with levels and salary ranges and runs about 80% of hiring through a portal (multiple-choice tests, a case assessment rated by AI) before a hiring manager steps in with AI notes on strengths and gaps.
- [00:48:55] Candidates may use AI on assessments; what is judged is whether their own thinking went into it, since AI output is only as good as the thought fed in.
- [00:51:48] Structured courses still have merit, but unstructured learning now matters more; use AI to access information, but do not hand your learning itself over to it.
- [00:56:05] Angel investing only works as a portfolio: expect most of ten bets to fail and one or two to return the lot; three bets is not angel investing. Of his 17 or 18, four exited, one returning capital and three doing well.
- [00:59:25] The common factor in his winners was the founders: pedigree, hunger and a real problem. Back the founder first; angel networks with analysts help with the rest.
- [01:01:52] Finance leaders join scaling companies in a squeeze, cleaning up the past while building for growth. His rule: start roughly 90% reactive, protect the 10% spent on prevention, and within three to six months the balance flips.
- [01:03:38] Hire at least a finance manager or controller around the seed round so gaps do not compound; bring in a CFO at Series A or about 100 million in revenue.
- [01:07:52] For SMEs below 40 or 50 staff, the biggest fix is keeping accounts current; lagging books mean lagging decisions, so review what is working daily.

## Stories and mistakes

- [00:12:08] He came to the UAE for one exploratory conversation in 2018 and 2019 and says it never ended; reading up on the region before that meeting is what convinced him.
- [01:00:50] Joining Flipkart, Ola and Noon as a finance leader meant about a year of cleaning up legacy issues while the business kept sprinting; Flipkart's first Big Billion Day was a whole-office effort.
- [01:04:55] At his Flipkart interview he asked what his job description would be; the CFO pointed at a blank whiteboard. It changed how he works: no boundaries, take the first priority in front of you.

## Where it fits the council

- Treasurer: keep books current daily, and hire a controller from seed so finance gaps do not compound.
- Capital Sceptic: banks fit you to their product; know whether a lender can read your cash cycle, and treat fees and penalties as the real cost.
- Safety Net: angel money only as a portfolio of ten or more, expecting most to fail and money locked up for years.
- Conscience: state the full cost upfront and avoid compounding penalties; Sharia as floor, ethics as ceiling.
- Long View: spend a protected slice on preventive work until it becomes most of the job; the region moved from stopover to headquarters base.
- Ambassador: back founders first, hire on demonstrated thinking, and publish salary ranges to earn trust.
