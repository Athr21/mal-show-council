# Ep 6 · Talal Tabbaa

Talal Tabbaa is CEO of CoinMENA, a crypto exchange licensed in Bahrain and the UAE that was recently sold to the Turkish exchange Paribu. He talks about building while employed, honest partnership, why he holds almost all his wealth in Bitcoin, and the 2022 crisis when the company nearly ran out of money. The second half covers how an exchange earns, payroll as a CEO's first duty, incentives in every deal, and what made CoinMENA sellable.

## Key takeaways

- [00:03:25] Building something on the side is not unethical if you still give the employer your full effort, the two do not compete, and you take no clients from your job. His model is Coinbase, built on weekends by an Airbnb engineer.
- [00:04:55] He did not start CoinMENA: a partner came up with the name and domain, and he first came in as an investor. A team needs complementary roles, and the CEO need not own the original idea; execution matters more than ideas.
- [00:07:00] Hard conversations only get resolved by having them; avoided, they fester and blow up. Even their investor raised that only two of three founders were active, so the founders agreed openly to play to strengths.
- [00:09:45] He will not wait for time to fix things. When unsure, act, collect the data, and let it steer the next move; assuming you already know is arrogance.
- [00:11:10] Commit fully to an outcome but keep a backup. Failure was ruled out at CoinMENA (go public, consolidate, or sell), yet the founders split about $1 million of capital so nobody borrowed, because a backup lets you keep making bets.
- [00:20:01] He would not go all in on Bitcoin if starting today; he bought early, with no dependants. Banks will not adopt it because it eats the inefficiency they earn from, the innovator's dilemma.
- [00:23:18] His case for Bitcoin is fixed supply: issuance does not respond to price and halves every four years, unlike wood or company shares, which expand when prices rise.
- [00:26:58] He saves in Bitcoin and treats everything else as speculation, capped at 1 to 2% of his invested portfolio. He sees much of the stock market as closer to a casino since 2008.
- [00:31:16] Trust in Bitcoin comes from incentives: anyone who could break it would win roughly a trillion dollars, and nobody has. An anonymous founder protected it from being shut down.
- [00:39:48] An exchange's first duty is safe custody and solvency, not price. CoinMENA caps weekly trading by user net worth and offers no leverage; he argues that platforms selling leverage cannot claim to protect users.
- [00:46:57] In 2022 CoinMENA burned through heavy marketing, lost a large share of its capital and nearly went under after FTX. An investor's $3 million round weeks later saved it, and it turned profitable in 2023.
- [00:49:28] Missing payroll is a CEO's biggest failure, because employees have families. Build rainy day reserves when profitable rather than cutting people at the first wobble.
- [00:50:29] In the crisis everyone took a graded pay cut, 50% for him down to 5% for the lowest paid, restored after three profitable months. Inefficiency still gets fired; fairness runs to staff and shareholders alike.
- [00:52:57] The best way to protect equity and staff options is a profitable business. He keeps CEO pay at about 20 times the average employee and expects AI to widen pay gaps.
- [00:58:29] Biggest mistakes: leaning on convertible notes and accepting a high liquidation preference, which cut the upside at exit. Hire a lawyer early.
- [00:59:55] What worked: promoting whoever stepped up rather than hiring on credentials, remote before COVID, referrals instead of paid ads, one salesperson coordinating outside agents, and a very lean team. Remote only works, he says, if the team is severely understaffed with redundancy planned inside it.
- [01:03:10] CoinMENA earns from a flat trading fee plus an FX spread. Its users are retail traders, companies moving stablecoins for payroll and transfers, long-term Bitcoin holders, and now regulated institutions.
- [01:05:51] He is against diversification for himself, calling it the route to mediocre results, but only after covering rent and family needs. He says this is not advice.
- [01:08:12] He avoids bonds: he thinks official inflation understates real costs, and debt sets lender against borrower while equity aligns partners.
- [01:11:44] He argues a dollar system built on government debt cannot be Sharia compliant, sees gold and possibly Bitcoin as candidates, but says he is not qualified to rule on Bitcoin.
- [01:19:00] To get acquired, run a profitable business, keep licences clean, and avoid an inflated valuation. Aim for a deal where team, investors and buyer all win, and make the company attractive enough to draw inbound offers.
- [01:22:10] He sold to Paribu, a cash-rich, self-funded Turkish exchange, over offers that were possibly richer, because he wanted to work with them and they know the region. The world is bigger than Silicon Valley.
- [01:24:31] Signatories must agree limits before negotiating. Selling takes far more CEO time than fundraising, and staff talk about options can wipe out productivity.
- [01:25:32] A near sale in 2022 collapsed at the last minute, but its deep diligence taught them what buyers want. Since then: tell only those who must know, and announce nothing until the money has landed.
- [01:28:30] For financial freedom, keep costs low, invest the surplus, and stop swapping time for money. Find an older mentor who wants to help.

## Stories and mistakes

- [00:15:11] At 23 he quit a fast-track PwC job for a Saudi family office role with no formal offer, a risk he could take because his family was his backup.
- [00:18:36] Relatives staged an intervention and his counsellor said he would never finish Purdue engineering; proving them wrong became his fuel.
- [00:46:57] Heavy 2022 marketing spend left CoinMENA close to bankruptcy after FTX; he lent personally to meet salaries until Beco's round arrived. The loan was signed by all parties as a convertible note and paid out at the exit.
- [00:50:29] Graded pay cuts across the whole team, reversed after one profitable quarter, kept trust intact.
- [00:58:29] Raising on convertible notes and a high liquidation preference reduced the exit payout; he skipped a lawyer early to save money.
- [01:10:29] Paying a swimming teacher on outcome rather than per class, his everyday example of aligned incentives.
- [01:16:01] A personal low in 2022 and the birth of his son in 2024 both happened while running CoinMENA; work kept him moving.
- [01:25:32] A sale fell through at the last minute in 2022, before FTX; the diligence from it made the later sale possible.
- [01:28:45] He lived in a shoebox flat in Switzerland with a mattress on the floor while saving aggressively.
- [01:31:01] He found his mentor by selling him Bitcoin with downside protection and asking for an hour a month in return; when the price dropped below the floor, he honoured the buy-back.

## Where it fits the council

- Treasurer: payroll first; build reserves while profitable and share any cuts from the top down [00:49:28].
- Capital Sceptic: watch liquidation preference and convertible stacks, and avoid inflated rounds that scare buyers [00:58:29].
- Safety Net: commit fully to the outcome but fund it without personal debt, so you can bet again [00:11:10].
- Conscience: do not sell leverage while claiming to protect users; cap exposure to what users can afford [00:39:48].
- Long View: profitable, clean and modestly valued companies attract inbound buyers; announce nothing until the money lands [01:19:00].
- Ambassador: choose the buyer you want to work with, not only the highest bid, and agree limits between signatories first [01:22:10].
