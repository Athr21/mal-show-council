# Ep 3 · Abdallah Abu-Sheikh

Abdallah Abu-Sheikh is a serial founder, with past companies in renewables, finance and super apps, who has just raised a large seed round to build Maal, an AI-native Islamic digital bank from the UAE. The host tests how venture pressure fits with faith-based limits, whether Islamic finance is more than relabelled interest, and how he thinks about money, security, giving and debt. Note: every line in the transcript is labelled "Speaker (unidentified)", so host and guest are told apart by sense throughout.

## Key takeaways

- [00:03:04] He builds in the region because he feels tied to its problems and you solve best what you are close to; waiting for outsiders to fix things is a habit he dates to the last century or so and sees fading.
- [00:06:23] People are polymaths by nature; he rejects the idea that a degree limits what you may try, and says you can lead work you are not a technical expert in.
- [00:08:29] Money is not the first need, a real problem is. Many founders have a solution looking for a problem, and capital belongs much later in the cycle.
- [00:10:31] A large Chinese robotics firm he visited prototypes in cardboard and tape many times before using metal; prototyping is a culture, and it is cheap.
- [00:11:12] He calls regional venture capital "vulture capital": good US funds were started by operators who back founders with experience, while many local funds invest other people's money and copy global theses.
- [00:14:30] VCs need humility, and founders should stop rewriting their plans on the say-so of a junior associate. Both sides need each other.
- [00:17:29] He frames Islamic finance as community benefit set against capitalism's focus on the individual, while stressing it is pro-wealth: the richer you are, the more you owe society.
- [00:20:20] Ethics belong to everyone, but religious rulings (Sharia) should stay with trained scholars, because one ruling can affect hundreds of millions of lives.
- [00:23:35] People have handed their ethics to departments and monthly charity; he says each person must answer for their own conduct.
- [00:24:58] He accepts that some institutions hide behind a halal label and can charge more as "profit" than conventional interest. Part of Maal's mission is to show how such rulings are made.
- [00:26:42] The problem Maal targets: products common elsewhere are missing in this region, especially lending to small and medium businesses and services for the unbanked.
- [00:28:21] On "why me", he feels a duty to try: no one in the US or China will solve Islamic finance for the region, and he would rather try and fall short than not try.
- [00:30:41] Criticism needs credentials: earn standing by doing the work first, as Ghazali studied philosophy deeply before critiquing it. He discounts most outside opinion.
- [00:34:20] A big raise should prompt motivation, not envy. He reminds people he once struggled to raise small sums and was told a business would fail.
- [00:36:56] There is no real security in health or money; work hard, but do not believe effort alone guarantees the result.
- [00:43:11] No single feature is a killer feature; advantages should stack up into a moat. Maal's bets are AI (lean and fast) and "productising ethics" through transparency.
- [00:44:04] AI-native means AI is in the data and technology architecture from day one, with fewer people in each loop. Incumbent banks struggle to switch because they would have to write off heavy past investment.
- [00:45:40] He advises skipping university if income is the goal: information is cheap, the best practitioners are not in academia, and he does not rate an MBA above any other candidate.
- [00:48:40] He hires on one test: here is a problem, show me a working prototype. AI lets anyone prototype, so slides are no longer enough.
- [00:49:52] Raising a big round does not change your ethics; if it does, you never had them. You chose the pressure, so do not complain about it.
- [00:53:07] Talent frustrates him more than regulators: people who do not know, people who think they know, and capable people paralysed by indecision.
- [00:57:48] If money is your motivation you will never have enough; contentment matters more than riches, and everyone needs a purpose beyond money.
- [00:59:24] Do not wait to be rich before giving. Giving most of a little shows more generosity than giving a small share of a lot.
- [01:03:44] Borrowers trapped in debt share responsibility for taking it on; Islamic finance's communal side is to help them out rather than send collectors.
- [01:05:54] Live within, or below, your means. He condemns social media wealth display and says people with real money do not flaunt it.
