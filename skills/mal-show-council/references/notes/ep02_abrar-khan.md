# Ep 2 · Abrar Khan

Abrar Khan runs CryptoWala, a bootstrapped company that teaches ordinary Indians to buy Bitcoin in small monthly amounts and hold it for years. The host presses him on volatility, custody, money laundering, tax, lending and the risk to lower-income families of pushing a 15-year-old asset. His case rests on Bitcoin as verifiable digital gold, discipline over trading, and financial education.

## Key takeaways

- [00:03:59] His million-dollar target is a market-size argument: if Bitcoin, at about $1.5 trillion, reaches gold's roughly $35 trillion, the price lands near $1 million. He admits the timing could be well off.
- [00:05:16] Gold's weakness, in his view, is that it is physical: recent war disruption showed it cannot move easily, and it cannot be carried across borders the way a phone wallet can.
- [00:08:37] Paper or bank-held gold still asks you to trust the custodian; he frames gold as a trust asset and Bitcoin as a verification asset you can check on a public ledger.
- [00:10:00] Bitcoin left on exchanges is not really yours ("not your keys, not your coins"), and people who keep it there are usually trading, not holding.
- [00:11:01] His analogy: you can be sure AI wins without knowing which AI company wins. For Bitcoin, you know the direction but not the year, so time in the market matters more than entry point.
- [00:12:16] A friend who bought a lump sum at the 2021 peak was still down five years later; buying monthly instead (dollar-cost averaging) would have roughly doubled his money by Abrar's estimate.
- [00:13:14] He treats Bitcoin as a pure store of value and every other coin as a company with founders and risk. Only someone who studies the market should put a slice into Ethereum; most people should not.
- [00:18:27] Pushed on stablecoins, he argues a dollar stablecoin still carries dollar inflation, since governments print to fund wars and stimulus.
- [00:20:00] He uses the three functions of money (exchange, store of value, unit of account) to argue fiat fails as a long-term store of value for retirees.
- [00:26:03] Price and fundamentals are separate: a large holder dumping coins would crash the price, but would not change the code or what Bitcoin does.
- [00:27:57] He calls banking a scam because banks lend out deposits many times over and pay savers far less than they charge borrowers; Bitcoin threatens that the way the internet threatened the post.
- [00:29:29] The host's strongest counter: a volatile currency cannot support industrial lending, since no factory can repay a loan whose value might double. Abrar's answer is a stablecoin backed by Bitcoin and lower volatility as the market grows.
- [00:34:03] On money laundering, he argues crime ran on fiat long before Bitcoin and weighs good uses against bad, as with the internet. The host separates tax evasion from trafficking and sanctions money.
- [00:38:29] He says he would pay tax only to a government he sees as fair, and does not see one today. The host argues tax funds education, health and defence and that undermining it harms nations.
- [00:40:16] He concedes Bitcoin has problems that he and most Bitcoiners cannot yet answer, and points to the code being upgradable, for example against quantum computing.
- [00:43:36] His advice to newcomers: do not start by reading about Bitcoin. Read how money, banks, tax and gold work first, find the flaws, then judge what Bitcoin claims to fix.
- [00:44:37] CryptoWala is launching a monthly investment plan (SIP) in India for Bitcoin and a few top coins only, with no meme coins, aiming for every Indian to hold at least a small amount.
- [00:53:25] The core discipline is mindset: his mother bought small amounts of gold for decades without watching the price. Anyone who wants to trade Bitcoin should stay out.
- [00:55:28] Playing it safe in fixed deposits loses money when deposit rates sit below inflation; he calls people educated but financially illiterate.
- [01:00:11] CryptoWala takes no sponsorships or paid collaborations so that people can trust it; education stays free.
- [01:01:15] The company is bootstrapped from his own money and Bitcoin gains, with nothing raised from outside. His first answer, "by myself", sits inside the host's line (transcript labels this Host).
- [01:03:12] Asked who he is beyond Bitcoin, he says do not trust him, verify, and admits he may be wrong on some details.
- [01:08:24] He holds almost everything in Bitcoin and says risk comes from not understanding something; with knowledge it becomes probability. He keeps money aside so the family can live without touching it for years.
- [01:10:11] His early losses on an initial coin offering and on Luna taught him to avoid coins that depend on a founder.
- [01:24:00] For a first step: block out an hour a day to study, stay away from trading, treat it professionally, and expect wealth over five to ten years, not quickly.

## Stories and mistakes

- [00:11:31] A friend who bought at the 2021 top messaged him, still at a loss five years on; the lesson he draws is to buy monthly, not in one go.
- [00:42:51] His path ran from airline ground staff to stockbroker, then a Bloomberg article in 2015 that started years of reading.
- [01:10:11] He lost money in initial coin offerings while young and chasing quick gains, then again on Luna despite rating its founder. Both pushed him to Bitcoin only.
- [01:16:08] He tells the story of Mansa Musa giving away so much gold on pilgrimage that Egypt's economy suffered, to argue value needs scarcity.

## Where it fits the council

- Treasurer: fixed deposits below inflation lose value (00:55:28); keep living money aside before locking anything away (01:08:24).
- Capital Sceptic: bootstrapped, no outside money and no sponsors (01:01:15, 01:00:11); the host's lending objection shows the limits of a volatile asset (00:29:29).
- Safety Net: going nearly all in on one asset is the case to challenge (01:08:24); custody and lost keys are on you (00:41:52).
- Conscience: pushing a volatile asset on lower-income families (00:50:00 question) and his stance on tax (00:38:29) both need a hard look.
- Long View: hold for five to ten years and buy monthly rather than time the market (00:12:16, 01:24:00).
- Ambassador: trust earned by "verify, don't trust" and free education with no paid promotion (01:03:12, 01:00:11).
