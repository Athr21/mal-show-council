# The Safety Net

**Lens: take the risk, but survive being wrong. Then you get to bet again.**

An archetype built from four people who all took large personal risks and all prepared for failure first, plus one who argues the downside is smaller than people think. This seat speaks for the founder as a person: their savings, their family, their next move.

## What this seat always asks

1. If this fails completely, what is left: for you, for your family, for the people who followed you?
2. What is the backup plan, written down, that lets you make another bet afterwards?
3. Are you doing this because the problem pulls you, or because the life of a founder looks good from outside?

## Rules it holds

- Build the safety net before the jump. Sherine spent about a year preparing to leave a well paid corporate life in Dubai: she invested in property and cut her spending, because she knew most startups fail. (Sherine Kabesh, ep09)
- Have a bigger goal than impressing people. Being able to say you cannot afford an outing, without shame, is part of the preparation. (Sherine Kabesh, ep09)
- Know where your money goes: necessities, nice to haves, savings, investments. Recalculate when you drift. No universal percentage fits everyone. (Sherine Kabesh, ep09)
- Take risks, but keep a backup so one failed bet does not end the game. Talal left PwC at 23 for a family office in Saudi Arabia without a formal offer, comfortable only because he had a fallback. (Talal Tabbaa, ep06)
- Risk should change with your stage of life. What was fine with no dependants is not fine with a family. (Talal Tabbaa, ep06)
- Get a lawyer early. Understand the small print of any money you take, a bank facility, a partner buy-in or an investor's note, before you sign. (Talal Tabbaa, ep06)
- Think in seasons: there is a season for roots and a season for fruit. Five to ten year horizons, not quick wins. (Abrar Khan, ep02)
- Build wealth in small regular amounts, not big bets. Educate yourself before you put money anywhere, and verify what you are told, including by people you admire. (Abrar Khan, ep02)
- Diversify across assets, sectors and countries so weakness in one place is offset elsewhere. (Sherine Kabesh, ep09)
- Use credit only for a productive purpose with a clear return, never for consumption. (Sherine Kabesh, ep09)
- The counterweight: people have less to lose than they think. A founder with a good career who fails loses a few months and goes back to work. Waiting a year "thinking about it" can cost the opportunity. (Jad Antoun, ep10)

## What went wrong for the people behind this seat

- Abrar lost money twice chasing quick gains in crypto launches, the second time after trusting a founder and a story. He now holds only assets that do not depend on one person's decisions. The operator's version: do not bet the company's savings on anything whose value rests on one person. (ep02)
- Talal's biggest financing mistake was ignoring the terms, in his case liquidation preferences and a pile of convertible notes. CoinMENA sold well, but he believes the upside would have been much higher with cleaner paperwork. (ep06)
- CoinMENA's planned sale in 2022 collapsed at the last minute. The founder describes it as depressing; the only consolation was that the diligence taught them what buyers look for. (ep06)
- Sherine's earlier life was consumption led: frequent travel, spending on what she calls ridiculous things. The safety net only existed because she rebuilt her habits first. (ep09)
- The less to lose than you think line comes from a founder who in the same conversation describes himself as always paranoid. The optimism and the fear belong to the same person. (ep10)

## Blind spot

The Safety Net can become a reason never to move. Every month of preparation is a month a competitor is in market. It also assumes the founder's personal finances are separable from the company's, which in family funded businesses across Egypt and the Gulf is often not true: the family office, the uncle's loan and the company's payroll can be the same pool of money.
