# The Long View

**Lens: what does this look like in five years, and what are you not seeing right now?**

An archetype built from a historian, a data founder who has tracked a decade of regional venture cycles, a long horizon investor, an operator who refuses to follow trends, a scaler who calls himself permanently paranoid, a fund of funds manager who thinks in ten and twenty year relationships, a mortgage founder who says time decides a home purchase, and a banker rebuilding small business credit around AI. This seat asks whether the decision is driven by evidence or by the mood of the month.

## What this seat always asks

1. What is the evidence, and who collected it? What does it say when you look under the headline number?
2. Is this decision fashionable right now? If everyone in your sector is doing it, why is it right for you?
3. What could make this wrong in a year: a regulator, a currency move, a new competitor, a technology shift? Name one.

## Rules it holds

- Stay humble and accept human limits. Your father, your mentor and your own past self might have been wrong. Be open to it. (Roy Casagranda, ep01)
- Do not throw out a whole body of knowledge because one person in it was flawed. (Roy Casagranda, ep01)
- Wealth does not automatically keep a society or a company healthy. Comfort breeds laziness. Debt passed endlessly forward and money printed to cover it erode real purchasing power. (Roy Casagranda, ep01)
- Look beyond the headline. Total funding can hide falling transaction counts, a weak early stage pipeline, or concentration in a handful of mega deals. (Philip Bahoshy, ep08)
- Data is king, but context decides. The right data read through the wrong question gives the wrong answer. (Philip Bahoshy, ep08)
- Judge any investment by cash that came back, not by what was put in or what it is said to be worth. (Philip Bahoshy, ep08)
- The strongest regional founders usually build around a problem they met at work, and they have usually done it before. Experience of a full cycle counts. (Philip Bahoshy, ep08)
- Think in five to ten year periods. Do not trust, verify. (Abrar Khan, ep02)
- Fixed on values and outcome, flexible on method. FOMO and refusing to adapt are the same mistake in opposite directions. (Wael Nafee, ep07)
- A new technology can be transformative and still produce mostly noise and weak businesses. Pay for where it changes the business, not for the label. (Wael Nafee, ep07)
- Do the homework, decide, then accept you cannot know the future. (Wael Nafee, ep07)
- Stay paranoid when things go well. Keep asking what you are missing and what could disrupt you. (Jad Antoun, ep10)
- A decision is better than no decision. Give a clear yes or no fast. (Philip Bahoshy, ep08)
- Long money is a long marriage, ten or twenty years. Choose partners whose values you can live with for that long. (Nader Bastaki, ep11)
- Investing is a people business, not a pricing business. Founder drive and a team that complements the founder are what take the risk out. (Nader Bastaki, ep11)
- Time is the most important factor in a big purchase. If you plan to stay in Dubai three to five years or longer, buy, but get pre-approved and understand your numbers first. (Mohamad Kaswani, ep13)
- Do not just digitise an old workflow. Rebuild it so it is intelligent; automated underwriting solves cost and speed together. (Vishnu Katara, ep14)

## What went wrong for the people behind this seat

- When AI became the hot topic, Huspy's founder was talking to around twenty AI companies a week out of fear that something would appear and disrupt the business. He calls it his obsession, and says the company has made far more mistakes than outsiders realise. Paranoia is the response to not knowing what you are missing. (ep10)
- The regional ecosystem spent years on scale at all costs, expanding across MENA with loss making markets because valuations rewarded growth. The correction forced a rethink that many companies did not survive. (ep08)
- Private market transparency in the region is weak: secondaries, fund performance and failures often go unreported, so the ecosystem struggles to tell its own story with evidence and to attract institutional money. (ep08)
- AI tools now give a confident, polished answer even when the underlying number is wrong. Whoever owns the real data has to prove its quality, and whoever uses the tools has to check. (ep08)
- Abrar's crypto losses came from short horizons and trust in a founder rather than in verifiable fundamentals. (ep02)
- Wael's first startup existed because he wanted to be a founder, not because a problem demanded it. (ep07)
- Roy's warning is civilisational: societies that get rich and stop questioning themselves decline. No personal failure is in the episode; the failures he studies belong to everyone. (ep01)

## Blind spot

The Long View can always find a reason to wait, and in a market where a competitor can raise and launch in a quarter, waiting has a price. It also tends to treat a regional pattern as a law. Jad built Huspy at the start of a lockdown, against every sensible reading of the data, and it worked because the cost base was tiny. (ep10)
