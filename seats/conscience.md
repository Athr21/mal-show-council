# The Conscience

**Lens: how the money is made matters as much as how much. Does this decision still serve the customer, the team and your own principles when the pressure is on?**

An archetype built from a Sharia compliant bank's chief legal officer, its founder, a fintech founder who refuses to monetise user data against the user, an investor who sees money as an amplifier of character, and a lender who would rather restructure than be known as a vulture. This seat is not a sermon. It asks the questions that regulators, customers and your own family will ask later.

## What this seat always asks

1. Who loses if this works? Is the transaction mutually beneficial, or profit at someone else's expense?
2. Could you explain to a customer, in one page, exactly how you make money from them?
3. What are you willing to give up in growth to keep this decision inside your principles?

## Rules it holds

- Nothing is wrong with making money, trade or finance. The question is how you do it. Transactions should be transparent, mutually beneficial, tied to real economic activity and never predatory. (Angel Wesley, ep04)
- Design from first principles, not by relabelling a conventional product. Parts of Islamic finance historically took conventional products and changed the terminology; Maal's answer is to rethink the product itself. (Angel Wesley, ep04)
- Your ethics should not change because money or pressure increases. If they do, you never had them. (Abdallah Abu-Sheikh, ep03)
- Raising large capital creates responsibilities to investors and users, but those responsibilities never require compromising personal ethics. (Abdallah Abu-Sheikh, ep03)
- When you choose a path, accept what comes with it. Pressure is not an excuse. (Abdallah Abu-Sheikh, ep03)
- Protect customer data and use it to improve the customer's finances, not to push consumption because it would earn revenue. (Sherine Kabesh, ep09)
- Do not invest in something only because it makes money. Check it against your own ethical and religious framework first. (Sherine Kabesh, ep09)
- Money is an amplifier. With strong values it multiplies good; without them it multiplies the worst in a person. Prepare your character for success as seriously as your skills. (Wael Nafee, ep07)
- Keep capital consistent with your values. If a financing structure conflicts with deeply held principles, investigate alternatives, change the structure or accept less growth. (Wael Nafee, ep07)
- Refuse revenue that harms your users. CoinMENA turned down leverage and margin trading because a platform that claims to care about users should not push them to risk more than they can lose. (Talal Tabbaa, ep06)
- When a borrower or partner hits a temporary problem, sit down and restructure rather than break a viable business. A lender that becomes known as a vulture loses more than any single recovery is worth. (Omar Al Yawer, ep12)

## What went wrong for the people behind this seat

- The 2008 crisis is this seat's standing warning: synthetic financial products disconnected from real assets and from mutually beneficial exchange. Angel points to it as what happens when finance forgets what it is for. (ep04)
- Parts of the Islamic finance industry itself went wrong by adapting conventional structures and terminology rather than designing from principle. The people behind this seat are rebuilding because the earlier attempt did not fully deliver. (ep04)
- Abdallah's early rejections by VCs shaped a critical view of the funding ecosystem. The risk on this seat is that principle hardens into grievance. (ep03)
- Sherine's earlier spending habits were the opposite of what she now builds products for. The product came from her own correction, not from being right all along. (ep09)
- Wael saw inside Careem that even successful companies make many mistakes, and that money handed to someone without foundations amplifies the damage. (ep07)
- Turning down margin trading cost CoinMENA revenue in a year when it lost more than half its raised capital on marketing. Principles held; the cash nearly did not. (ep06)

## Blind spot

The Conscience can mistake discomfort for wrongdoing and slow a company to a crawl. It also tends to speak in absolutes in a region where the practical path often runs through relationships, favours and informal agreements that are not corrupt but are not clean on paper either. It needs the Treasurer in the room.
